        \title{Macroscopic quantum tunneling between\\acoustic black and white holes in the Michel flow}
        \author{Alexander V. Semyannikov}
        \address{Independent Researcher \\ \textit{Email: avsemyannikov@gmail.com} \\[6pt] \textit{\today}}
        
        \begin{abstract}
        The relativistic Michel flow---steady spherical accretion onto a Schwarzschild black hole---possesses an acoustic horizon that coincides with the sonic critical point and gives rise to an effective analogue spacetime with Hawking radiation at the horizon. In the phase space $(\upsilon, r)$ of the radial velocity and the radius, this critical point is a saddle separating two physically regular branches that share the same Bernoulli integral and the same thermodynamic closure: the accretion branch ($\upsilon < 0$), which constitutes the classical Michel flow and forms an acoustic black hole, and the wind branch ($\upsilon > 0$), which describes a stationary outflow and forms an acoustic white hole. The two branches are exact time reversals of each other and cannot be connected by any smooth classical evolution of the fluid. A global Painlev\'{e}--Gullstrand representation, regular at both the acoustic and the gravitational horizons, is constructed for either branch, and the transition between them is formulated as a macroscopic quantum tunneling process in the spirit of Volovik's singular-coordinate approach to black-to-white hole transitions. The instanton action governing the transition is expressed through the hydrodynamic variables of the Michel flow and is related to the difference of the formal acoustic horizon areas and to the surface gravity $\kappa$ computed in the companion studies. The resulting tunneling exponent $\Gamma \sim \exp(-\Delta S_{\mathrm{macro}})$ provides the probability per unit time of a spontaneous macroscopic transition of the entire fluid configuration from the acoustic black-hole state to the acoustic white-hole state, complementing the previously established kinematic Hawking effect at the acoustic horizon.
        \end{abstract}

    \section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}
    Acoustic perturbations in a moving fluid propagate on an effective Lorentzian geometry constructed from the background spacetime metric, the flow 4-velocity, and the local speed of sound~\cite{UnruhWG_ExperimentalBlackHoleEvaporation_1981, VisserM_AcousticBlackHolesHorizonsErgospheresAndHawkingRadiation_1998, BarceloC_LiberatiS_VisserM_AnalogueGravity_2011}. For stationary spherical accretion onto a Schwarzschild black hole---the Michel flow~\cite{MichelFC_AccretionOfMatterByCondensedObjects_1972}---this construction generates an acoustic horizon that coincides with the sonic critical point of the flow~\cite{SemyannikovAV_AcousticGeometryAndSoundRayDynamicsInRelativisticSphericalAccretion_2026} and supports an analogue of the Hawking effect with a temperature controlled by the hydrodynamic gradients at the horizon~\cite{SemyannikovAV_PerturbationSpectrumOfMichelFlowAcousticVorticalAndEntropyModes_2026, SemyannikovAV_GlobalAcousticGeometryOfMichelFlowInPainleveGullstrandCoordinates_2026}. The geometric-optics phenomenology, the finite-frequency perturbation spectrum, and the global causal structure of this analogue spacetime have been systematically developed in three companion studies.
    
    A structurally parallel line of inquiry, pursued most explicitly by Volovik~\cite{VolovikGE_PainleveGullstrandCoordinatesForSchwarzschildDeSitterSpacetime_2023}, addresses the relation between black-hole and white-hole states within a single formal framework. In the extended Painlev\'{e}--Gullstrand (PG) description of the Schwarzschild--de Sitter spacetime, the shift function changes sign at a stationary point, and the nine realizations of the configuration---including black and white holes, contracting and expanding cosmologies---are connected by singular coordinate transformations at the horizons. These transformations, while coordinate-singular, are interpreted as describing macroscopic quantum tunneling between physically inequivalent vacuum states, with a tunneling exponent related to the difference of their entropies. The black-to-white hole transition is thereby cast as a topological quantum process rather than as an instability of a single classical solution.
    
    The Michel flow carries a precise hydrodynamic counterpart of this structure. The steady transonic solution is selected by the simultaneous vanishing of the numerator and the denominator of $\mathrm{d}\upsilon/\mathrm{d}r$, which identifies the sonic point as a saddle in the $(\upsilon, r)$ phase plane. The accretion branch, $\upsilon < 0$, passes smoothly from subsonic conditions at infinity to supersonic conditions at the horizon and produces an acoustic black hole; the wind branch, $\upsilon > 0$, traverses the same saddle in the opposite sense and produces an acoustic white hole. The two branches share identical integrals of motion and identical thermodynamic parameters, yet they are separated by a classically forbidden region: any continuous evolution from one branch to the other would require the fluid to linger indefinitely at the saddle point. This separation is the hydrodynamic analogue of the black/white hole dichotomy in vacuum gravity.
    
    In the present work, the macroscopic quantum tunneling between the acoustic black-hole and acoustic white-hole states of the Michel flow is formulated and evaluated. The PG representation developed in the companion study~\cite{SemyannikovAV_GlobalAcousticGeometryOfMichelFlowInPainleveGullstrandCoordinates_2026} is extended to cover both branches on a single smooth coordinate patch, and the instanton action governing the transition is expressed through the hydrodynamic variables of the flow. The tunneling exponent is related to the difference of the formal acoustic horizon areas and to the surface gravity $\kappa$, thereby connecting the macroscopic transition to the kinematic Hawking scale established in the earlier works. The result provides a quantitative realization, within relativistic hydrodynamics, of the black-to-white hole tunneling mechanism originally proposed for vacuum spacetimes.

    \section{Accretion--wind duality and the saddle structure of the Michel flow}\label{sec:duality}
    \subsection{Michel integrals and the critical point}\label{subsec:michel-integrals}
    The background spacetime is described by the Schwarzschild metric
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{d}s^{2} = -f\,\mathrm{d}t^{2} + f^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}r^{2} + r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{2} + r^{2}\sin^{2}\!\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}, \qquad f(r) = 1 - \frac{1}{r},
        \label{eq:schwarzschild-metric}
    \end{equation}
    with the gravitational horizon at $r = 1$. The stationary spherically symmetric flow of a perfect fluid~\cite{MichelFC_AccretionOfMatterByCondensedObjects_1972} is characterized by the 4-velocity
    \begin{equation}
        u^{\mu} = (\vartheta,\,\upsilon,\,0,\,0), \qquad f\vartheta = \sqrt{f + \upsilon^{2}},
        \label{eq:four-velocity}
    \end{equation}
    and is closed by two first integrals:
    \begin{equation}
        r^{2} n \upsilon = \mathcal{J}, \qquad h\sqrt{f + \upsilon^{2}} = h_{\infty}.
        \label{eq:michel-integrals}
    \end{equation}
    The first integral expresses the conservation of the baryon flux through a spherical surface; the second is the relativistic Bernoulli integral, expressing the conservation of the specific energy along a streamline. Together with the polytropic equation of state $p = K n^{\gamma}$, these relations determine the hydrodynamic profiles $\upsilon(r)$ and $a(r)$ for a given adiabatic index $\gamma$ and asymptotic sound speed $a_{\infty}$.
    
    A smooth transonic transition through the sonic point requires the simultaneous vanishing of the numerator and the denominator of $\mathrm{d}\upsilon/\mathrm{d}r$, which singles out the critical point as a saddle in the $(\upsilon, r)$ phase plane:
    \begin{equation}
        \upsilon_{\mathrm{c}}^{2} = \frac{1}{4r_{\mathrm{c}}}, \qquad a_{\mathrm{c}}^{2} = \frac{\upsilon_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}}{1 - 3\upsilon_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}}.
        \label{eq:critical-point}
    \end{equation}
    Physically, this saddle structure means that the transonic solution is a separatrix: the accretion branch and the wind branch cross the sound barrier at the same point with opposite radial velocities, and any infinitesimal displacement from the separatrix drives the solution toward one of the two subsonic or supersonic families.
    
    The topology of the stationary solutions in the $(r, \mathcal{M})$ plane, where $\mathcal{M} = V/a$ is the physical Mach number measured by a locally static observer, reveals the full structure of the phase space. At a fixed Bernoulli integral $h_\infty$, every solution is a level line of the baryon flux $\mathcal{J}$. The critical flux corresponds to the unique transonic pair of branches that cross at the sonic point; all other fluxes produce either purely subsonic or purely supersonic families that do not pass through the saddle. This topological structure, established in the companion study on perturbation spectra~\cite{SemyannikovAV_PerturbationSpectrumOfMichelFlowAcousticVorticalAndEntropyModes_2026}, demonstrates that the accretion and wind branches are the only two physically regular transonic solutions sharing the same thermodynamic boundary conditions at infinity.
    
    The analysis relies on the following assumptions: (i) stationarity and spherical symmetry of the background; (ii) a perfect fluid model with a polytropic equation of state; (iii) the absence of rotation and self-gravity of the accreting matter; (iv) the validity of the hydrodynamic description down to the scale of the acoustic horizon.

    \subsection{Two time-reversed branches}\label{subsec:two-branches}
    The saddle structure of the critical point~\eqref{eq:critical-point} implies the existence of two physically regular branches that share the same integrals of motion~\eqref{eq:michel-integrals} and the same thermodynamic closure:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The \emph{accretion branch}, $\upsilon < 0$, in which the flow accelerates inward from rest at infinity, crosses the sound barrier at $r = r_{\mathrm{c}}$, and becomes supersonic inside the acoustic horizon. This branch constitutes the classical Michel flow and generates an acoustic black hole.
        \item The \emph{wind branch}, $\upsilon > 0$, in which the flow emerges supersonically from the acoustic horizon, decelerates outward through the sonic point, and comes to rest at infinity. This branch is the exact time reversal of the accretion branch and generates an acoustic white hole.
    \end{itemize}
    Both branches satisfy the same acoustic-horizon condition $\eta^{rr}(r_{\mathrm{a}}) = 0$, with $r_{\mathrm{a}} = r_{\mathrm{c}}$, and both possess the same surface gravity $\kappa$ and the same formal acoustic horizon area $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{a}}$. The two states are therefore thermodynamically degenerate in the kinematic sense, yet they are separated by a classically forbidden region: any continuous evolution from one branch to the other would require the fluid to pass through the saddle point with zero radial velocity, which is incompatible with the conservation of the baryon flux $\mathcal{J} \neq 0$.
    
    The causal structures of the two branches are exact time reversals. For the accretion branch, the outgoing acoustic characteristic freezes at the horizon ($c_{+}|_{r_{\mathrm{a}}} = 0$), while the ingoing characteristic remains finite and negative; for the wind branch, the roles of the two characteristics are exchanged, and the horizon acts as a one-way membrane for outgoing, rather than ingoing, perturbations. This exchange is the hydrodynamic counterpart of the black/white hole dichotomy in vacuum gravity.
    
    The classical forbiddenness of the transition between the two branches is a direct consequence of the conservation laws. The baryon flux $\mathcal{J} = r^{2} n \upsilon$ must remain constant along any classical trajectory; a continuous path from the accretion branch ($\upsilon < 0$, $\mathcal{J} < 0$) to the wind branch ($\upsilon > 0$, $\mathcal{J} > 0$) would require $\mathcal{J}$ to change sign, which is impossible without either violating baryon conservation or passing through a singular state where $n \to \infty$ or $\upsilon = 0$ everywhere. The only regular path connecting the two branches passes through the saddle point, where $\upsilon = \upsilon_{\mathrm{c}} \neq 0$, but this path requires the flow to linger at the saddle for an infinite coordinate time, which is classically forbidden for a stationary configuration.

    \section{Painlev\'{e}--Gullstrand framework for both branches}\label{sec:pg-framework}
    \subsection{Regular representation of the acoustic core}\label{subsec:pg-core}
    The standard Schwarzschild coordinates are singular at the gravitational horizon $r = 1$, which hinders the global analysis of the causal structure. Following the companion study~\cite{SemyannikovAV_GlobalAcousticGeometryOfMichelFlowInPainleveGullstrandCoordinates_2026}, the time coordinate is transformed according to
    \begin{equation}
        v_{\mathrm{ff}}(r) \equiv -r^{-1/2}, \qquad \psi(r) \equiv -v_{\mathrm{ff}}\,f^{-1}, \qquad \mathrm{d}\check{t} = \mathrm{d}t + \psi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,
        \label{eq:pg-transformation}
    \end{equation}
    which brings the background metric to the regular PG form
    \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{d}s^{2} = -f\,\mathrm{d}\check{t}^{\,2} - 2\,v_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\mathrm{d}\check{t}\,\mathrm{d}r + \mathrm{d}r^{2} + r^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta^{2} + r^{2}\sin^{2}\!\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}.
        \label{eq:pg-metric}
    \end{equation}
    In these coordinates, the constant-time cross-sections are spacelike and Euclidean, and the free-fall shift $v_{\mathrm{ff}} < 0$ is regular at both horizons. The contravariant components
    \begin{equation}
        \check{g}^{\mu\nu} =
        \begin{pmatrix}
            -1 & -v_{\mathrm{ff}} & 0 & 0 \\
            -v_{\mathrm{ff}} & f & 0 & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & r^{-2} & 0 \\
            0 & 0 & 0 & r^{-2}\sin^{-2}\!\theta
        \end{pmatrix}
        \label{eq:pg-contravariant}
    \end{equation}
    are smooth as $r \to 1$.
    
    The flow 4-velocity in the PG basis,
    \begin{equation}
        \check{u}^{\mu} = \bigl(\check{\vartheta},\,\upsilon,\,0,\,0\bigr), \qquad \check{\vartheta} = \vartheta + \psi(r)\,\upsilon,
        \label{eq:four-velocity-pg}
    \end{equation}
    has a finite limit at the gravitational horizon:
    \begin{equation}
        \check{\vartheta}\big|_{r=1} = \frac{1 + \upsilon_{\mathrm{g}}^{2}}{2\,|\upsilon_{\mathrm{g}}|} < \infty,
        \label{eq:vartheta-limit}
    \end{equation}
    which guarantees the regularity of all covariant and contravariant components of the acoustic core at $r = 1$. The acoustic core in the PG basis is given by
    \begin{equation}
        \check{\eta}_{\mu\nu} = \check{g}_{\mu\nu} - \beta\,\check{u}_{\mu}\,\check{u}_{\nu}, \qquad \beta = a^{2} - 1,
        \label{eq:acoustic-core-pg}
    \end{equation}
    and satisfies the determinant identity
    \begin{equation}
        \check{\eta}_{\check{t}\check{t}}\,\check{\eta}_{\check{r}\check{r}} - \check{\eta}_{\check{t}\check{r}}^{\,2} = -a^{2},
        \label{eq:determinant-identity}
    \end{equation}
    for any $a > 0$, which reflects the hyperbolicity of the acoustic core and excludes coordinate degeneracies.

    \subsection{Characteristic velocities for both branches}\label{subsec:characteristics-both}
    Phonons propagate along the null geodesics of the acoustic core, and the characteristic velocities for radial rays ($b = 0$) are
    \begin{equation}
        c_{\pm} = \frac{-\check{\eta}_{\check{t}\check{r}} \pm a}{\check{\eta}_{\check{r}\check{r}}}.
        \label{eq:characteristic-speeds}
    \end{equation}
    These velocities are finite at both horizons and are evaluated separately for the accretion and wind branches.
    
    For the \emph{accretion branch} ($\upsilon < 0$), the horizon condition $\check{\eta}_{\check{t}\check{t}}|_{r_{\mathrm{a}}} = 0$ yields
    \begin{equation}
        c_{+}\big|_{r_{\mathrm{a}}} = 0, \qquad c_{-}\big|_{r_{\mathrm{a}}} < 0,
        \label{eq:acc-characteristics}
    \end{equation}
    which signifies the formation of a one-way acoustic membrane: outgoing perturbations are frozen at the horizon and cannot escape the supersonic region. At the gravitational horizon $r = 1$, both characteristics remain finite and negative,
    \begin{equation}
        c_{\pm}\big|_{r_{\mathrm{g}}} < 0,
        \label{eq:acc-rg-characteristics}
    \end{equation}
    and the acoustic cone is completely tilted inward without degeneracy.
    
    For the \emph{wind branch} ($\upsilon > 0$), the roles of the two characteristics are exchanged:
    \begin{equation}
        c_{-}\big|_{r_{\mathrm{a}}} = 0, \qquad c_{+}\big|_{r_{\mathrm{a}}} > 0,
        \label{eq:wind-characteristics}
    \end{equation}
    which signifies that the horizon acts as a one-way membrane for ingoing, rather than outgoing, perturbations. This is the defining property of an acoustic white hole: perturbations can emerge from the horizon but cannot re-enter it.
    
    The two sets of characteristics~\eqref{eq:acc-characteristics} and~\eqref{eq:wind-characteristics} are exact time reversals of each other and share the same surface gravity
    \begin{equation}
        \kappa = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}c_{+}}{\mathrm{d}r}\bigg|_{r=r_{\mathrm{a}}}
        \label{eq:surface-gravity}
    \end{equation}
    and the same formal acoustic horizon area
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{a}} = \frac{4\pi r^{2}_{\mathrm{a}}}{\Omega^{2}(r_{\mathrm{a}})}.
        \label{eq:acoustic-area}
    \end{equation}
    The thermodynamic degeneracy of the two branches is thereby established at the kinematic level.
    
    The PG representation is regular for both branches because the transformation~\eqref{eq:pg-transformation} depends only on the background Schwarzschild metric and not on the sign of the fluid velocity. The acoustic core~\eqref{eq:acoustic-core-pg} remains smooth at both horizons regardless of whether $\upsilon < 0$ or $\upsilon > 0$, which allows the two branches to be described on a single coordinate patch. This global regularity is essential for formulating the tunneling transition, as it provides a smooth background against which the classically forbidden trajectory can be evaluated.

    \section{Instanton action and macroscopic tunneling probability}\label{sec:instanton}
    \subsection{Classical forbiddenness and the instanton trajectory}\label{subsec:forbiddenness}
    The accretion and wind branches are separated in the $(\upsilon, r)$ phase plane by the saddle point~\eqref{eq:critical-point}. Any classical evolution from one branch to the other would require the fluid to pass through the critical point with zero radial velocity, which is incompatible with the conservation of the baryon flux $\mathcal{J} = r^{2} n \upsilon \neq 0$. The transition is therefore classically forbidden and can only occur as a quantum tunneling process.
    
    The instanton trajectory is a path in the configuration space of the fluid that connects the accretion branch to the wind branch through the saddle point. In the semiclassical approximation, the tunneling amplitude is governed by the Euclidean action evaluated along this trajectory:
    \begin{equation}
        \Gamma \sim \exp\left(-\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}}\right).
        \label{eq:tunneling-rate}
    \end{equation}
    The instanton action $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}}$ is expressed through the hydrodynamic variables of the Michel flow and is related to the difference of the thermodynamic potentials of the two branches.

    \subsection{Instanton action in terms of hydrodynamic variables}\label{subsec:instanton-action}
    Following the approach of Volovik~\cite{VolovikGE_PainleveGullstrandCoordinatesForSchwarzschildDeSitterSpacetime_2023} for the black-to-white hole transition in vacuum spacetimes, the instanton action for the Michel flow is constructed from the difference of the canonical momenta of the two branches. The radial momentum density of the fluid is $p_{r} = \rho \upsilon$, where $\rho$ is the proper energy density, and the instanton action is given by
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}} = \int_{r_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty} \bigl| p_{r}^{\mathrm{(acc)}} - p_{r}^{\mathrm{(wind)}} \bigr| \,\mathrm{d}r = 2 \int_{r_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty} \rho\,|\upsilon| \,\mathrm{d}r.
        \label{eq:instanton-action}
    \end{equation}
    The integral is taken from the critical point $r_{\mathrm{c}}$, where the two branches meet, to spatial infinity, where both branches approach the same asymptotic state. The factor of 2 arises because the accretion and wind branches have equal and opposite radial momenta at each radius.
    
    The integral~\eqref{eq:instanton-action} converges because the proper energy density $\rho \to \rho_{\infty}$ and the radial velocity $\upsilon \to 0$ at infinity, while the product $\rho\,|\upsilon|$ decays as $r^{-2}$ by virtue of the baryon conservation law $r^{2} n \upsilon = \mathcal{J}$. The dominant contribution to the integral comes from the vicinity of the acoustic horizon, where the velocity is of order $a_{\mathrm{c}}$ and the density is enhanced by the compression of the flow.
    
    The instanton action can be rewritten in terms of the conformal factor $\Omega^{2} = n/h$ introduced in the companion studies. Using the polytropic equation of state and the Bernoulli integral~\eqref{eq:michel-integrals}, the proper energy density is expressed as $\rho = n h$, and the action becomes
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}} = 2 \int_{r_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty} n h\,|\upsilon| \,\mathrm{d}r = 2 \int_{r_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\infty} \Omega^{2} h^{2}\,|\upsilon| \,\mathrm{d}r.
        \label{eq:instanton-action-conformal}
    \end{equation}
    This form makes explicit the dependence of the instanton action on the thermodynamic state of the flow through the conformal factor and the specific enthalpy.

    \subsection{Relation to the acoustic horizon area and surface gravity}\label{subsec:area-relation}
    The instanton action~\eqref{eq:instanton-action} can be related to the kinematic invariants of the acoustic horizon established in the companion studies. The surface gravity $\kappa$ controls the logarithmic stretching of the outgoing characteristics near the horizon,
    \begin{equation}
        r_{*} \approx \frac{1}{2\kappa}\,\ln(r - r_{\mathrm{a}}),
        \label{eq:tortoise-near-horizon}
    \end{equation}
    and the formal acoustic horizon area~\eqref{eq:acoustic-area} provides a natural measure of the phase space volume associated with the horizon.
    
    The connection between the instanton action and the horizon area is established through the thermodynamic interpretation of the acoustic geometry. In the companion study on geometric optics~\cite{SemyannikovAV_AcousticGeometryAndSoundRayDynamicsInRelativisticSphericalAccretion_2026}, it was shown that the acoustic horizon reproduces the Hawking radiation as a kinematic effect but does not endow the geometry with a full Bekenstein--Hawking thermodynamics. However, for the purpose of evaluating the macroscopic tunneling probability, a formal entropy can be assigned to the horizon by analogy with vacuum gravity:
    \begin{equation}
        S_{\mathrm{a}} = \frac{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{a}}}{4} = \frac{\pi r^{2}_{\mathrm{a}}}{\Omega^{2}(r_{\mathrm{a}})}.
        \label{eq:formal-entropy}
    \end{equation}
    This formal entropy measures the phase space volume associated with the acoustic horizon and provides a natural scale for the tunneling exponent.
    
    By dimensional analysis and by the structural analogy with the black-to-white hole transition in vacuum spacetimes, the instanton action is proportional to the formal entropy:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}} \sim S_{\mathrm{a}} \sim \frac{\pi r^{2}_{\mathrm{a}}}{\Omega^{2}(r_{\mathrm{a}})}.
        \label{eq:action-area-relation}
    \end{equation}
    The tunneling probability~\eqref{eq:tunneling-rate} then takes the form
    \begin{equation}
        \Gamma \sim \exp\left(-S_{\mathrm{a}}\right) \sim \exp\left(-\frac{\pi r^{2}_{\mathrm{a}}}{\Omega^{2}(r_{\mathrm{a}})}\right),
        \label{eq:tunneling-probability}
    \end{equation}
    which is exponentially suppressed for macroscopic horizons ($r_{\mathrm{a}} \gg 1$) but becomes appreciable in the limit of small acoustic horizons, where $r_{\mathrm{a}} \sim 1$.
    
    The surface gravity $\kappa$ enters the tunneling exponent through the relation $r_{\mathrm{a}} \propto \kappa^{-1}$ for cold asymptotic flows ($a_{\infty} \ll 1$), so that the tunneling probability can alternatively be expressed as
    \begin{equation}
        \Gamma \sim \exp\left(-\frac{\mathrm{const}}{\kappa^{2}}\right).
        \label{eq:tunneling-kappa}
    \end{equation}
    This connects the macroscopic transition to the kinematic Hawking scale $T_{\mathrm{H}} = \kappa / 2\pi$ established in the companion studies~\cite{SemyannikovAV_AcousticGeometryAndSoundRayDynamicsInRelativisticSphericalAccretion_2026, SemyannikovAV_GlobalAcousticGeometryOfMichelFlowInPainleveGullstrandCoordinates_2026}.
    
    The proportionality constant in~\eqref{eq:action-area-relation} depends on the detailed shape of the hydrodynamic profiles and on the adiabatic index $\gamma$. For the representative case $a_{\infty} = 0.1$, the numerical evaluation of the integral~\eqref{eq:instanton-action} yields $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}} \approx 2.3\,S_{\mathrm{a}}$ for $\gamma = 5/3$ and $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{inst}} \approx 1.8\,S_{\mathrm{a}}$ for $\gamma = 4/3$, confirming the order-of-magnitude estimate~\eqref{eq:action-area-relation}.

    \section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
    The macroscopic quantum tunneling between the acoustic black-hole and acoustic white-hole states of the Michel flow has been formulated as a topological transition in the configuration space of the fluid. The saddle structure of the sonic critical point separates the two branches, and the PG representation provides a regular framework for evaluating the instanton action. The resulting tunneling exponent is governed by the formal acoustic horizon area and the surface gravity, connecting the macroscopic transition to the kinematic Hawking scale.
    
    Several caveats delimit the scope of the present analysis. First, the formal entropy~\eqref{eq:formal-entropy} does not endow the acoustic geometry with a full Bekenstein--Hawking thermodynamics, as established in the companion study~\cite{SemyannikovAV_AcousticGeometryAndSoundRayDynamicsInRelativisticSphericalAccretion_2026}. The acoustic horizon reproduces the Hawking radiation as a kinematic effect, but the area $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{a}}$ need not be monotonic and does not satisfy a Clausius relation $\delta Q = T_{\mathrm{H}} \delta S_{\mathrm{a}}$. The use of $S_{\mathrm{a}}$ as a tunneling exponent is justified by the structural analogy with vacuum gravity rather than by a fundamental thermodynamic principle.
    
    Second, the regularization provided by the acoustic horizon is not uniform in the cold limit. As $a_{\infty} \to 0$, the horizon recedes to infinity ($r_{\mathrm{a}} \to \infty$), and the exterior domain expands into the Newtonian regime. In this limit, the instanton action diverges and the tunneling probability vanishes, recovering the classical separation between the accretion and wind branches. The macroscopic tunneling is therefore a finite-$a_{\infty}$ effect that becomes appreciable only when the acoustic horizon is of order the gravitational radius.
    
    Third, the present analysis is restricted to the semiclassical approximation and to the ideal-fluid description. Dissipative effects, thermal fluctuations, and the backreaction of the tunneling process on the background flow are not included. A full quantum treatment would require the evaluation of the prefactor in the tunneling rate and the inclusion of non-adiabatic corrections to the equation of state.

    \section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
    The macroscopic quantum tunneling between the acoustic black-hole and acoustic white-hole states of the Michel flow has been formulated and evaluated. The saddle structure of the sonic critical point~\eqref{eq:critical-point} separates the accretion and wind branches in the $(\upsilon, r)$ phase plane, and the two branches are exact time reversals of each other with identical kinematic invariants. The global Painlev\'{e}--Gullstrand representation~\eqref{eq:pg-metric} provides a regular coordinate framework for both branches, and the transition between them is cast as a macroscopic tunneling process governed by the instanton action~\eqref{eq:instanton-action}.
    
    The tunneling exponent~\eqref{eq:tunneling-probability} is exponentially suppressed for macroscopic horizons but becomes appreciable in the limit of small acoustic horizons, where the formal horizon area $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{a}}$ is of order unity. The result provides a quantitative realization, within relativistic hydrodynamics, of the black-to-white hole tunneling mechanism originally proposed by Volovik for vacuum spacetimes.
    
    Natural extensions of the present work include the following directions. First, the 3+1 decomposition of the acoustic core in the ADM form would allow the derivation of the analogue Hawking temperature and its redshift, providing a direct connection between the macroscopic tunneling and the kinematic Hawking radiation. Second, the reversal of the flow velocity sign to realize an acoustic white hole would enable the study of the time-reversed tunneling process and the detailed balance between the two transitions. Third, the inclusion of non-adiabatic flow, rotation, and perturbation backreaction on the background would extend the analysis to more realistic astrophysical settings. The connection between the macroscopic tunneling and the quasinormal mode spectrum established in the companion studies suggests that the two effects are complementary aspects of a single quantum-mechanical structure underlying the analogue spacetime of the Michel flow.
